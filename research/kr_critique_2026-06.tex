\section*{Summary}

This note responds to the latest feedback round: expand the corpus toward
electorally consequential legislation (bills that move votes and donors,
bipartisan ``secret congress'' laws, and failed bills with a record of
\emph{why} they failed), and critique/refine the knowledge representation so
that agent queries are fast and the under/overcoverage problem shrinks. Three
things were built: (1) a \textbf{passage-dynamics data layer} (roll-call
votes, party splits, derived outcomes) covering all 232 corpus bills, fetched
entirely from keyless public endpoints; (2) a new \textbf{\code{secret\_congress}
topic corpus} of 29 verified exemplar bills, from the CHIPS and Science Act to
the failed Border Act of 2024; (3) three \textbf{aggregate query primitives}
(\code{count\_bills}, \code{get\_bill\_votes}, \code{find\_bills\_by\_outcome})
plus a per-topic \textbf{facet-coverage contract} in \code{corpus\_coverage()}.
The critique below explains why these specific pieces, and what remains open.

\section{What the representation could not say before tonight}

The schema's strength has been \emph{textual} fidelity: verbatim sections,
canonical citations, local definitions, reified amendments. Its blind spot was
\emph{political} fidelity. Concretely, before this round the system could not
answer any of the following, on any bill:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item Did this bill pass? (No outcome field anywhere.)
  \item Was the vote bipartisan? (No vote data at all; cosponsor party
        counts were the only proxy, and cosponsorship is a weak proxy ---
        the Laken Riley Act had near-zero Democratic cosponsors and 12
        Democratic Senate votes.)
  \item Why did this bill fail? (Action trails existed for only the
        $\sim$30\% of AI bills that had Congress.gov enrichment; zero for
        redistricting.)
  \item How many bills are in the corpus from the 119th Congress? (The
        known eval failure: no count primitive, so agents paginated search
        results and counted --- the dominant error source in the
        Opus-4.7-85\%/Sonnet-4.6-70\% gap.)
\end{itemize}

The meeting notes ask for exactly this layer: ``how much it affected the
vote,'' ``show negative bills that failed (why it fails?),'' ``a lot of
legislation is passed on a bipartisan vote in a single bill.'' These are not
text questions; they are \emph{passage-dynamics} questions, and the
representation had no nouns for them.

\section{The data layer: \code{billstatus.json}}

One enrichment file per bill directory, written by \code{ingest/billstatus.py}
and backfilled by \code{scripts/backfill\_billstatus.py}. Three keyless
sources: GovInfo BILLSTATUS bulk data (status, actions, structured
sponsor/cosponsors, subjects, public-law numbers, recorded-vote pointers),
House Clerk roll-call XML (per-party totals, per-member positions), Senate
LIS roll-call XML (per-member positions, totals derived). No API key exists
in the environment right now; none is needed for any of this.

Design decisions worth defending:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Derived outcome with evidence.} \code{outcome} is a
        13-value enum (\code{enacted}, \code{failed\_cloture},
        \code{died\_in\_committee}, \code{reported\_no\_floor\_vote},
        \code{passed\_house\_only}, \ldots) derived from the action trail
        and roll-call results, never asserted bare: an \code{events} list
        carries each dated, verbatim action string behind the label. An
        agent asked ``why did the Border Act fail?'' quotes the cloture
        vote and its date instead of confabulating a narrative.
  \item \textbf{Bipartisanship as a number, labels second.}
        \code{bipartisan\_support} $=\min$ over the two major parties of
        that party's yea-share on the vote, in $[0,1]$. Labels
        (\code{party\_line} $<0.10\le$ \code{cross\_party} $<0.50\le$
        \code{bipartisan} $<0.90\le$ \code{near\_unanimous}) are
        display sugar; the number is what filters and sorts. Calibration
        checks: CHIPS final House concurrence (24 R yeas) $\to 0.11$;
        the COMPETES shell vote (1 R yea) $\to 0.005$; PACT Act Senate
        $\to$ bipartisan.
  \item \textbf{Member positions only for outcome-determining votes.}
        Passage/cloture/veto-override votes carry all $\sim$435 member
        positions (party, state) --- this is what enables delegation
        geography (``how did California vote on CHIPS?''). Amendment and
        procedural votes keep party totals only, bounding file sizes on
        amendment-heavy vehicles (NDAAs run 40+ roll calls).
  \item \textbf{The vehicle-bill trap is documented, not hidden.} A
        vehicle bill carries roll calls from an earlier life under another
        name (H.R.~4346's 2021 votes are America COMPETES votes), and
        conversely a bill's famous vote can live on a \emph{different}
        bill number (KOSA's 91--3 Senate vote was on the S.~2073 vehicle,
        so S.~1409 itself correctly shows no floor vote --- the curator
        note carries the explanation). The system prompt and tool
        descriptions instruct the agent to check vote dates/questions
        before attributing margins; \code{bills.bipartisan\_support} uses
        the \emph{final} passage-class vote for the same reason.
\end{itemize}

\section{The corpus expansion: \code{secret\_congress}}

29 bills, hand-curated and \emph{individually verified} against BILLSTATUS
(title + public-law number) before inclusion --- bill numbers were never
trusted from memory; the one ambiguous case (DISCLOSE Act) was resolved to
S.~4822 (117th) by checking the failed 49--49 cloture record. Six clusters
defined in \code{corpus/secret\_congress\_criteria.md}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Cluster & $n$ & Exemplars \\
\midrule
\code{quiet\_bipartisan\_law} & 8 & OSRA, postal reform, PACT, WRDA 2024, TAKE IT DOWN \\
\code{high\_salience\_bipartisan\_law} & 7 & CHIPS, IIJA, Safer Communities, Laken Riley \\
\code{bipartisan\_but\_died} & 7 & Border Act, KOSA, Tax Relief Act, SAFE Banking, DISCLOSE \\
\code{absorbed\_into\_vehicle} & 2 & USICA/Endless Frontier, Electoral Count Reform Act \\
\code{omnibus\_vehicle} & 2 & FY2023 omnibus, WRDA-2022$\to$NDAA \\
\code{party\_line\_contrast} & 3 & IRA, OBBBA, Secure the Border Act \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Notes on fidelity to the meeting notes: the ``FRONTIERS Act'' is the
\emph{Endless Frontier Act} (S.~1260, 117th), included with its real fate
(passed the Senate 68--32 as USICA, died in conference, science core enacted
inside CHIPS); the ``water infrastructure bill'' appears twice (S.~914, the
89--2 standalone, and H.R.~7776, WRDA-as-NDAA-vehicle); the donor angle is
carried by the DISCLOSE Act (campaign-finance disclosure, failed cloture
49--49 on party lines) and by the antitrust pair (AICO, JCPA) that died
without floor votes under platform lobbying. The single-bill bundling
phenomenon is directly queryable: ECRA is \code{absorbed\_into\_vehicle} and
H.R.~2617 is its \code{omnibus\_vehicle}.

The clusters are curatorial; the vote record is mechanical. An agent that
distrusts a cluster tag can recompute from \code{get\_bill\_votes}. This
two-layer design (curated interpretation over mechanical evidence) follows
the schema's existing derivation-typing principle for edges.

\section{Query speed: aggregate-first, count-closing}

The eval's failure mode was never retrieval quality --- it was \emph{shape}:
questions whose answer is a set or a number were being served by a
paginated-search loop. Each loop iteration costs a round trip
($\sim$1--3\,s of model latency) and risks page-boundary undercounting.
The three new primitives close the shapes that remained open:

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item \code{count\_bills(group\_by, topic, congress, outcome, \ldots)} ---
        exact SQL aggregate, one call, $\sim$10\,ms. Directly closes eval
        Q6 (``how many bills from the 119th?'').
  \item \code{find\_bills\_by\_outcome(outcome, cluster, min/max\
        bipartisan\_support, \ldots)} --- the set-valued passage-dynamics
        query (``which bipartisan bills failed?'').
  \item \code{get\_bill\_votes(bill\_id)} --- one bill's full fate in one
        call: outcome, public law, events, votes with party splits.
\end{itemize}

These follow the precedent of \code{find\_bills\_defining} /
\code{find\_bills\_amending}: \emph{when the question shape is known, give
the agent a primitive that returns the complete answer with a coverage
note, so ``N results'' is the whole truth rather than page one of it.}

\section{Under/overcoverage: coverage as a contract}

The deeper refinement is in \code{corpus\_coverage()}. Previously it
reported corpus-level metadata (congresses, tiers, known gaps as prose).
It now reports, per topic, a \textbf{facet-coverage table}: for each data
facet (\code{actions}, \code{cosponsors}, \code{subjects}, \code{votes},
\code{outcome}, \code{embeddings}), how many of the topic's bills carry
that facet, computed live from the index so it cannot drift.

The point is epistemic, not cosmetic. Under- and overcoverage errors are
both \emph{coverage-belief} errors: the agent either believes the corpus
knows less than it does (abstains wrongly --- undercoverage of the answer
space) or believes it knows more (asserts from a facet that is only
30\%-populated --- overcoverage). A live facet table converts both into
checkable propositions: ``\code{votes} covers 25/29 secret\_congress
bills, 6/12 redistricting bills, and 3/191 ai\_governance bills (most AI
bills die in committee --- no roll call exists); \code{outcome} covers
232/232; embeddings cover 0 bills in this branch's
\code{--skip-embeddings} build (7{,}880/29{,}616 ai\_governance sections
were embedded on the main checkout before the pass was handed off).'' The agent's
honesty rules can then be mechanical: before aggregating over a facet,
check the facet is total; when it is not, say which slice the answer rests
on. This is the same move the schema made for definitions (gap nodes
instead of silent misses), applied to the corpus itself.

Two genuine overcoverage risks now exist and are guarded: vehicle-bill
votes (\S2) and partial dense-embedding coverage (the dense leg of hybrid
search silently favors embedded bills; FTS covers all bills, and the facet
table now exposes the imbalance instead of hiding it).

\section{Geography and temporal}

Both feedback items are served by existing fields once votes exist:
\code{vote\_positions(state, party, position)} answers delegation
questions and state-level vote maps for the policymaker-facing visual
layer; BILLSTATUS \code{subjects} include state names where bills touch
them; \code{introduced\_date}, action dates, vote dates, and
\code{congress} give the temporal spine. A dedicated geography aggregate
(``votes by state delegation'') would be a natural fourth primitive if the
frontend wants it; the SQL is a two-line GROUP BY on tables that now
exist.

\section{What is deliberately not claimed, and what is next}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Media salience is asserted, not measured.} The
        secret-congress thesis is about coverage volume; the corpus
        encodes salience only through my cluster curation. A salience
        facet (e.g., GDELT or newspaper-archive mention counts per bill)
        would make \code{quiet\_bipartisan\_law} falsifiable. Medium
        effort; high value for the thesis.
  \item \textbf{``How much it affected the vote'' (electoral outcomes) is
        not yet linkable.} Connecting member positions to subsequent
        vote-share changes needs election-results data (MIT Election Lab)
        and a member-district crosswalk, plus real identification care ---
        this is a research design, not an ingest script. Flagged for a
        design discussion before building.
  \item \textbf{Donor data (FEC) is out of scope tonight.} The donor angle
        is currently represented by donor-\emph{related} legislation
        (DISCLOSE) rather than donor behavior data.
  \item \textbf{Embeddings remain partial} (deferred to Raj's box per the
        earlier handoff); the new topic adds $\sim$130k sections to that
        queue, dominated by the four mega-vehicles.
  \item \textbf{Senate roll-call coverage for pre-1989 style URLs,
        amendment-level Senate votes inside BILLSTATUS,} and a handful of
        unresolved vote refs are recorded per-bill in
        \code{unresolved\_vote\_refs} rather than dropped silently.
\end{itemize}
